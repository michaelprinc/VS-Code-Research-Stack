# Report templates

Copy the skeleton, then delete sections the question does not need.

## Contents
- Short orientation answer
- Narrative literature review
- Structured review
- Research log
- Evidence matrix

## Short orientation answer (1-2 pages)

```markdown
# [Topic]: a short orientation

**Question.** [one sentence]

**What the scholarship says.** [3-5 paragraphs organised by position, not by source]

**Where scholars disagree.** [the actual disagreement, and why]

**Confidence.** [Well-established / Likely / Conflicting / Limited, with a reason]

**Sources used.** [Chicago; each marked with access status]

**Not covered.** [languages, databases and periods not searched]
```

## Narrative literature review

```markdown
# [Specific title reflecting the question and scope]

## Executive summary
[Findings · major interpretive positions · strength and limits of the evidence · the key unresolved issue]

## 1. Question and scope
[question, definitions, boundaries, stated assumptions]

## 2. Method
[what was searched, with which tools, on what date, selection criteria, what was not searched]

## 3. State of scholarship
[major works, schools, developments - and why each matters here]

## 4. Findings and competing interpretations
[organised thematically or chronologically; counterevidence included; the synthesis is labelled as the synthesis]

## 5. Critical evaluation
[where conclusions are well supported, where they are tentative]

## 6. Gaps and next steps
[missing evidence, underrepresented voices, language and geography limits]

## 7. Conclusion
[direct answer, proportionate to the evidence]

## Bibliography
[primary sources separated from secondary scholarship when that helps; unverified entries marked]
```

## Structured review (only if the procedure is really followed)

Add to the above, in section 2: databases searched · search date and coverage period · every search string and variant · inclusion and exclusion criteria · screening and deduplication steps · full-text availability and reasons for exclusion · limitations including inaccessible databases and languages not searched. If any of these were skipped, call it a structured review, not a systematic review, and do not mention PRISMA.
