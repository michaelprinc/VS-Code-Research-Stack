What each step asserted:

1. **Routine repair of an unmodified 2.0 install.** `planInstall` → `upgrade {from: "2.0", to: "2.1"}`,
   all 14 files `outdated`, `forceOverwrites: []`. `repairBundle` → `previousVersion "2.0"`,
   `bundleVersion "2.1"`, `repair.drift: []`, `archive.reason "upgrade"`, `archive.files 14`;
   the workspace file hashes to the catalog sha256 `5dac3db8…` and the archived copy hashes to the
   2.0 fixture hash `7a446e42…` — the replaced bytes are in
   `.research-toolkit/bundles/archive/<bundleId>/<stamp>-<sequence>/`, not discarded.
2. **The same repair against a copy the operator edited.** The operator's bytes survive
   byte-for-byte (`1c0a7488…`), the file is reported in `repair.drift` as `user-modified`, and the
   archive holds the 13 files that *were* entitled to replacement.
3. **Confirmed force update** (`force: true`): `archive.reason "force-update"`, `archive.files 1`,
   the workspace now hashes to the reviewed `5dac3db8…`, the archived copy hashes to the operator's
   `1c0a7488…`.
4. **Restore.** `restoreArchive` puts `1c0a7488…` back in the workspace, archives what it displaced
   (the reviewed 2.1 bytes, `5dac3db8…`) with `reason "restore"`, and `listArchives` reports three
   archives newest-first: `restore`, `force-update`, `upgrade`, `refused: []`.

One nuance the test records rather than hides: after restoring a *force-update* archive of the same
bundle version, the receipt's `bundleVersion` stays `2.1` — `replacedVersion` is only recorded when a
run replaced a *different* version, so a same-version archive restores a user's copy of 2.1, not an
older release. `receipt.restore.archiveId` / `restoredVersion` and the per-file `restoredFrom` are
asserted instead.
